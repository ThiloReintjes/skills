# Presentation guidance

Apply the core writing workflow to the argument across slides and to the message within each slide. Determine whether the audience will hear a presenter, read the deck independently, or do both.

## Build the storyline before the slides

Write the sequence of takeaway headlines in plain text first. A headline states what the audience should conclude from the slide. For example, with supporting data, “Most cancellations happen before activation” gives a message that “Retention analysis” leaves unstated.

Read the headlines in order. The sequence should answer the audience's question, establish the decisive reasons, account for material uncertainty, and make any requested decision clear. An executive decision deck will usually put the recommendation early. Add context where understanding depends on it. A title page or navigation slide can use a topic label.

Choose the number of slides from the requested time or length and the reasoning needed. Preserve a requested slide count; combine closely related points or move supporting detail to notes when necessary.

## Give each slide a claim and support

For each substantive slide, identify:

- The main takeaway, expressed as a sentence headline.
- The evidence or explanation needed to justify that exact takeaway.
- The source and qualification necessary to interpret the support correctly.

Match the display to its job: a chart for a quantitative pattern, a table for options on common criteria, a diagram for a process or mechanism, or a short example for an unfamiliar idea. Text is sufficient when it communicates the point clearly. Choose visual evidence that carries information; decorative imagery earns no space by itself.

The headline's scope and confidence must match the body. Keep material qualifications visible on the slide. Put secondary methodology and sources in a readable footer, notes, or appendix. Use comparable scales, units, denominators, and time periods. If a chart requires missing data, identify the data needed or use an honest qualitative explanation. Do not supply imagined values or a chart-shaped placeholder as evidence.

## Adapt to how the deck is used

**Live presentation:** keep visible text short enough to follow while listening. Let the speaker supply examples and transitions in notes. Include enough context to interpret the slide's evidence. Keep decision-critical uncertainty visible.

**Read-ahead or leave-behind:** include enough explanation for an absent reader to follow each inference. Use concise paragraphs, annotations, and source notes where needed. A headline and unexplained chart rarely stand alone.

**Both:** keep a clear live layer and place additional explanation in notes or a companion document when the delivery format supports it. Critical qualifications belong with the claim in every version.

For a requested storyline, deliver slide titles and enough evidence, display guidance, and notes to make each slide buildable. For a requested deck file, create and inspect the artifact using the appropriate tools; a storyline alone does not complete the request.

## Verify the deck

Check that reading only the headlines preserves the argument, each slide supports its headline, and the sequence reaches the stated outcome. When slides are rendered, inspect legibility, hierarchy, clipping, and whether the evidence is understandable at the intended viewing size. Fix a crowded slide by simplifying its job, redistributing content, or moving secondary detail before shrinking type.
